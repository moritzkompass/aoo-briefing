What this changes

Ad groups and landing pages belong on back pain, neck and shoulder, sciatica and nerve pain, joint pain, and sleep and stress. Bidding on fibromyalgia buys clicks the practice cannot convert. Google's own topic analysis of the profile says the same thing: back pain relief sits at the top with 31 mentions.

03

Three things on the website that waste every ad dollar

  1. 01
    There is not a single tappable phone link on the homepage.

    The number appears four times as plain text in the source. From a phone, no visitor can call directly from the page. The phone call is this practice's strongest conversion, and the website contributes nothing to it.

  2. 02
    The booking button sends new patients into the returning-patient flow.

    The homepage links seven times to a path explicitly built for previous visits. Someone arriving from an ad, ready to book, lands in the wrong form.

  3. 03
    One page serves every condition.

    Someone searching for sciatica lands on a general overview. That costs ad quality, raises the cost per click, and loses visitors who were ready to make an appointment.

The good news

Everything after the click already works. 14.2 percent of clicks become a phone call, about twice the healthcare benchmark. The substance is there. What is broken can be fixed in days.

04

Every critical review comes down to the same thing

Out of 320 reviews, five are critical. Three of those carry written feedback, and all three point at one issue: the price was not clear before treatment.

My mother politely asked how much the session cost, but they never responded, avoiding the question. 1 star, verbatim
I felt pressured to buy supplements they claimed would help me. 2 stars, verbatim

The sore spot, and the biggest opening

Price comes up in 36 reviews overall. Meanwhile the one local competitor running serious search ads promotes a first consultation at $45 instead of $150, right in the ad copy. A landing page that states the price range openly repairs a real weakness and answers that offer at the same time.

06

What we build next

  1. 01
    A dedicated landing page for the search ads

    No navigation, a tappable phone number, a working booking path, and a short multi-step form. Built on the real treatment photos from the clinic rather than stock imagery.

  2. 02
    One version per condition that actually exists

    Back pain first, then neck and shoulder, sciatica and nerve pain, joints.

  3. 03
    Two immediate fixes to the current site

    Make the phone number tappable and point the booking button at the right path. Small effort, measurable right away, independent of everything else.

  4. 04
    Account audit and campaign rebuild

    Verify the campaign type, cut wasted search terms, align ad scheduling with clinic hours, switch from metro targeting to a radius, and get call tracking measured properly.

  5. 05
    The cheapest lever sits outside the advertising

    43 calls produced 10 patients. The other 33 left no trace, because nothing captures them. At this patient value, that is the largest single line item on this page.

07

The landing page, section by section

Ten sections, each with one job and one proof. Structure and hero design are built; copy fills in as today’s open questions are answered.

  1. 01
    “You have been through the tests. You are still in pain.”

    The opening confirms the search in four seconds: rating, the statement, one button, the phone number, and the treatment-room photo with the sclerotome chart on the wall.

  2. 02
    What we treat, and what you have already tried

    Back, neck and shoulder, sciatica and nerve pain, joints. Then the history our patients share: rising doses, a surgery, therapy that faded, clean scans.

  3. 03
    Who treats you

    Dr. Chang, her Doctorate in Oriental Medicine and Master’s in Biomedical Science, and her own line: medically tested, scientifically proven.

  4. 04
    What others say

    4.9 from 322 Google reviews with the reply rate, the three-year award with its publisher, and two patient voices about how she explains and listens.

  5. 05
    How it works, what it cannot do

    The mechanism in plain language with published evidence, the honest limits of the method, and the warning signs that belong in an ER, not here.

  6. 06
    Your first visit

    What happens, how long it takes, and the treatment plan phases in Dr. Chang’s own framework.

  7. 07
    What it costs

    The price of a first visit, superbills and HSA/FSA, and the promise that answers every critical review: you hear the price before you book.

  8. 08
    Before you ask

    Six questions sorted by fear, starting with safety and “does it hurt”, ending with cost and scheduling.

  9. 09
    The next step

    A two-field form, name and phone, with everything else optional. Consent line built for Florida telemarketing law. The phone number stands beside it as an equal path.

  10. 10
    Find us

    Address, hours and directions on a dark closing band, both ways in one last time.

Then, one page per condition

Once this page runs, it becomes the template for back pain, neck and shoulder, sciatica and joints, each with its own headline, its own patient voices and its own ad group.

08

What we need to settle today

Ordered by urgency. The first four block the build.

01What does a first treatment cost, and what does a typical care plan cost?blocking

No document, call, or script we reviewed contains a single figure. The page has to answer the price objection and we will not invent a number.

02How should people book, and is there a path for new patients?blocking

The current button leads into the returning-patient flow. Without the right destination, every optimization upstream is wasted.

03How is insurance handled?blocking

For US patients this is the second question after price. It appears nowhere in the material we have.

04May we use the treatment photos, and do signed authorizations exist?blocking

There are 50 strong clinic photos. Faces are almost never identifiable, but website use still requires written HIPAA authorization from the patients shown.

05Who answers the phone, and what happens to callers who do not book?revenue

33 lost calls in a single month. Five points of close rate is worth about a dollar off the cost per click, and it costs nothing to gain.

06What is a patient worth across a full course of treatment?revenue

We have been working with roughly $550. In conversation the figure of a few thousand dollars for a full treatment came up. This determines what a new patient may cost to acquire.

07Access to the Google Ads account

Without it the audit stays guesswork. The current pattern suggests an automated campaign type, which would explain most of the high cost per click.

08What happens to calls outside clinic hours?

The clinic is staffed Monday to Friday, 8am to 6pm. Ads run around the clock unless a schedule is set.
